Soft robotics

I research microfluidics in the Bioinspired Advanced Manufacturing Lab at the University of Maryland. I iterate on fluidic actuator and microchip designs, and I test microfluidic diodes and transistors that will eventually be used to build a soft robotic hand.

Below are one of my actuator designs in SolidWorks, a printed resin actuator of a similar design at rest and actuated, and my microchip design. The chip is 3D printed in resin and gives a micro- to mesoscale interface, which makes the diodes and transistors easier to post-process and test.

Miniature arm controller

For the UMD Loop Rover Team, I worked with eight other students to produce a design option for a miniature arm controller (MAC). The MAC controls the rover's arm. Because it is a directly scaled-down replica of the arm, it makes the arm easier to control.

I did most of my team's CAD in SolidWorks and was most involved with the base and its interface with the rest of the MAC. The interface lets the arm rotate freely and stay resilient under loading over time.

Over terrain vehicle

Seven other students and I built an over terrain vehicle (OTV) that could drive autonomously to a pylon, through an obstacle course, and to a destination zone.

I modeled the vehicle's parts and assembled them in Fusion 360. After the first build, we analyzed how the components performed and kept improving the design.

Wooden I-beam

Designed a wooden I-beam to withstand a point load in a universal testing machine. Calculations in Python gave a strength-to-weight ratio of over 2,300:1. I compared the predicted failure load with the actual failure load of 1,520 lbf and identified the failure mode.
